BPExch account types: user, dealer, master, super master

There are four BPExch account types, in this order — user, dealer, master, super master — each one issuing accounts to the tier below. Most people only ever need the first. But if you are being offered a "master account" by a man in a WhatsApp group, you should know exactly what each tier can do — and what it cannot.

Quick answer

A user account plays. A dealer account issues user accounts under itself. A master oversees dealers, and a super master sits at the top of its line. Each tier earns commission on the turnover below it and sets limits for the accounts under it.

Read the float column properly: that money is working capital the tier keeps in the system so its players' deposits credit and winnings pay without drama. It is a float, not a fee — nobody legitimate "sells" you a tier for it, and it is never paid to a stranger as the price of a title. When a seller quotes a wildly different float for the same tier, or asks you to send the float to a personal wallet as an "activation", you are not being onboarded. You are being harvested.

The order matters more than the titles

Titles inflate in the telling. What does not inflate is the chain: your account was issued by someone, and that someone answers to someone. When a problem lands — a disputed settlement, a payout question — it travels up the chain you actually have, not the one on a business card. This is why we tell players to open their BPExch account through a desk they can reach daily instead of the grandest title they can find: a responsive dealer beats an absent master every day of the week.

Thinking of becoming a dealer?

The honest version: dealing is a service business wearing a betting costume. Your players will message during matches, blame you for wallet outages, and judge you entirely on two things — how fast deposits credit and how fast winnings pay. The capital side is real too: your line's limits and your float have to cover a bad weekend for your players, which is a good weekend for their book only if you have the depth to pay it. If that still sounds attractive, the route in is through the tier above you: a master who will set your limits and your commission. Talk to the desk through the contact page and we will tell you plainly whether your player base justifies it — including when the answer is "not yet".

Red flags when someone offers you a tier

  • A "super master" offered for a small one-time fee to a stranger. Real lines are granted on trust and volume, not sold in groups.
  • Commission percentages that change depending on what you ask. The split is a number, agreed once, visible in your panel.
  • No answers about who sits above them. Every legitimate tier can name its upline; secrecy points the wrong way.
  • Pressure to recruit players before your own limits are even set. The cart is not just before the horse — there is no horse.

The fastest way to judge any tier offer — or any desk, including ours — is against published criteria rather than titles: the compare page lines the whole niche up on fees, payout behaviour and safety rules.

Questions we get asked about this

What is the difference between a dealer and a master account?

A dealer issues accounts to players and earns on their turnover. A master sits above dealers — issuing dealer accounts, setting their limits and earning across all their lines. Masters typically run several dealers rather than serving players directly.

Can a normal player hold a master account?

Technically an account can be issued to anyone, but the tier only makes sense with dealers beneath it. A 'master' with no line is a title without income, and anyone selling it as an upgrade for a player is selling the title.

How do I become a BPExch dealer?

Through the tier above: an existing master sets your limits and commission and issues your dealer login. Expect questions about your player base and your float — a master who asks neither is not protecting his line, and that should worry you.

Does my account type affect my odds or limits as a player?

Your odds are the exchange's — the same ladder for everyone. Your personal betting limits are set within your line, which is one reason a well-run desk matters: limits set by people who know your play beat defaults set by strangers.
