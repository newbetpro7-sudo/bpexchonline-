BPExch commission explained, with real rupees

BPExch commission explained in one sentence: a small percentage of your net winnings in a market, charged only when you win that market. The word still generates more support messages than any other on the statement — usually because nobody explained it before the first charge appeared. Here it is, plainly, with the arithmetic done in the open.

Published 23 October 2026 · Accounts · 5 min read

Quick answer

BPExch commission is a small percentage — set in your account terms, commonly in the 2–5% range — charged on your NET winnings in a single market, only when you win that market. It is never charged on deposits, withdrawals, stakes, or losing markets. Win a market by Rs 1,000 at 5% and the commission line reads Rs 50; lose the market and the line does not exist.

Three worked examples

Market resultNet winCommission at 5%You keep
Back bet wins, profit Rs 2,500Rs 2,500Rs 125Rs 2,375
Back and lay trades net out aheadRs 640Rs 32Rs 608
Market lost, −Rs 1,000—Rs 0−Rs 1,000 (the loss only)

Two details in that table matter more than the rate. First, net: if you backed and laid the same market and finished Rs 640 ahead overall, commission is charged on Rs 640 — not on each winning bet inside it. Second, per market: winning the match-odds market and losing the session market means commission on the first and none on the second; one does not cancel the other.

What commission is not

  • Not a deposit or withdrawal fee. Money in and money out carry no desk commission — anyone charging you a "processing fee" to release a payout is running a scam, full stop.
  • Not charged on turnover. Bet Rs 50,000 across a match and win Rs 300 net, and the charge is on the Rs 300.
  • Not negotiable after the fact — but it IS set in advance: your rate sits in your account terms, and dealer-tier accounts set rates for their own users, which is one of the how BPExch works details worth understanding before you accept any account from anyone.

Seeing it on your statement

Commission appears as its own line type after a market settles — the fourth line in the statement anatomy we break down in reading your BPExch statement. If a commission line ever looks wrong, do the net-win arithmetic for that market first; nine times in ten the line is right and the memory of the trades is what was fuzzy. For the tenth time, the statement is the shared evidence both you and the desk argue from.

Price it before you bet

The desk's own odds calculator carries a commission slider for exactly this reason: set it to your rate and every back, lay and green-up figure it shows is net of commission — the number you would actually keep. It is the only calculator in this niche that bothers, which tells you something about how the others expect you to find out.

Commission sirf jeet par lagta hai, woh bhi market ke net profit par. Haar par koi commission nahi, deposit ya withdrawal par kabhi nahi.
