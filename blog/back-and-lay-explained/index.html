Back and lay betting explained — with one PSL over

Back and lay betting explained properly starts mid-match, not with definitions. It is the powerplay, Lahore are batting, and you have Rs 1,000 and an opinion. Here is what backing does, what laying does, and what the famous "trade out" actually did to your money — ball by ball.

Published 16 October 2026 · Learning · 7 min read

The scene

Lahore Qalandars are 34 for 0 after four overs against Karachi. The ladder on the how BPExch works page shows Lahore at 1.96 to back and 1.97 to lay. You think they are flying and this price is generous. You back Lahore with Rs 1,000 at 1.96. If Lahore win, you collect Rs 960 profit; if they lose, your Rs 1,000 is gone. That is backing — the bet every punter already understands, just at a price set by other players instead of a bookmaker.

Ball three changes everything

Two wickets in three balls. Lahore are suddenly 36 for 2, the crowd has gone quiet, and the ladder has moved: Lahore now 3.40 to back, 3.45 to lay. Your Rs 1,000 back bet is in trouble. On a bookmaker's site, your options are prayer and a cash-out figure the house chooses. On the exchange, you have a third door: lay Lahore yourself.

The lay that locks the door

Laying Lahore at 3.45 means betting against them — taking the other side from someone who still believes. Suppose you lay with a backer's stake of Rs 410. Your position now reads: Lahore win, and your original back pays +960 while your lay loses 410 × (3.45 − 1) = Rs 1,004 — net roughly −44. Lahore lose, and your back loses 1,000 while your lay wins the backer's 410 — net roughly −590… which shows the amateur version of trading out: any random lay amount just makes a new mess. The desk's rule is simpler — lay enough that both outcomes land near the same figure. Here that means a lay stake around Rs 570 at 3.45, which leaves you roughly Rs 390 down if Lahore win and roughly Rs 430 down if they lose: a certain, survivable loss instead of a coin-flip between +960 and −1,000. When the price had moved your way instead, the same maths locks a profit on both sides — the position every exchange player calls "green all over".

What laying really is, in one paragraph

When you lay, you are the bookmaker for that slice of the market: you take a backer's stake, and your liability — stake × (price − 1) — is what you pay if their outcome lands. That is why the confirmation screen shows liability in big letters, and why laying at big prices with money you cannot afford is the fastest way to a bad night. Lay small, lay deliberately, and always read the liability line: it is the only number on the screen that can surprise you.

The beginner's first month, prescribed

  • Back only, at prices you understand, until the ladder stops looking like a spreadsheet and starts looking like a story.
  • Place one deliberate lay in week two — small, at a short price — purely to watch the liability and settlement do their work.
  • Trade out exactly once, badly, on purpose, with Rs 100. The lesson costs less than this article's pride.
  • Keep a notebook line per bet: price, reason, result. Exchange players who last are bookkeepers who bet, not punters who hope.
