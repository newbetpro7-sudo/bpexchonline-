BPExch dealer not replying? Read this before you panic

A BPExch dealer not replying is a horrible feeling: money in an account, a match tonight, and a WhatsApp chat that has gone from blue ticks to grey. Take a breath. Silence has several causes, they are not equally likely, and the order in which you do things now decides whether this ends as an anecdote or a loss.

Published 26 October 2026 · Safety · 5 min read

First, the causes — ranked by how often the desk sees them

Life, first. Dealers are people with phones that break, families that fall ill and — in this country — load-shedding. A day of silence from a dealer with a year of history is usually a day, not an exit. Number trouble, second. SIMs get blocked and phones get replaced; dealers reappear on a new number and announce it through their usual channels. Upline trouble, third. Your dealer may be stuck because their master has gone quiet on them — the chain problem described on the BPExch account types page — and they are stalling while they chase. And yes, the worst one exists: a small minority vanish with balances. It is real, it is not the most common explanation, and everything below is designed so you are protected even if it is yours.

Do these, in this order

  1. Stop sending money. Obvious, and yet: no "one last deposit to keep the account active". There is no such requirement, ever, and silence plus a deposit request is the scam's favourite costume.
  2. Gather your evidence while it is fresh. Screenshot the chat, your account statement, and every wallet confirmation (with TIDs) connected to this dealer. Screenshot your balance inside the account too. Evidence gathered today is worth ten reconstructions next month.
  3. Try the account itself. Sign in. If the account opens and the balance is there, your problem is a person, not your money — a crucial distinction. Note whether deposits or withdrawals are the blocked part.
  4. Use the other doors. The dealer's other number, their page, the group they run, the master above them if you know who it is. Keep it factual: ID, amount, dates. Public, factual posts get answers; abuse gets blocked.
  5. Give it a defined window. Forty-eight hours for a reply on a balance question is generous and reasonable. Say so in your last message: calm, dated, on the record.

What never to do

Never pay a "recovery agent" who appears in your DMs offering to unlock or retrieve the balance for a percentage — this is the second act of the same scam, and it feeds on exactly your situation. Never share your password with someone "from support" who has come to help; the real desk never needs it, including during a BPExch password reset. And never open a fresh account with the same silent dealer's "new desk" until the old balance is resolved in writing — your evidence and your leverage are the same thing.

When it resolves — and when it does not

If the dealer resurfaces with an explanation, judge the explanation by one test: does the money move? A same-day small withdrawal, honoured without commentary, reboots trust better than any apology — the same one-week test we recommend for judging any provider on the compare page. If the window closes in silence, withdraw what you can through any channel that works, then open fresh through a desk whose help pages, safety rules and track record you can inspect in daylight — and keep your future balances sized to what you could shrug off for a week. That last habit, unglamorous as it is, is the difference between a bad dealer and a bad month.
