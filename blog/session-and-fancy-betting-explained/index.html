Session betting explained: fancy, lambi and PSL lines

Session betting explained, finally — because every South Asian newcomer meets the same wall: the match-odds market makes sense, and then there is a screen of lines — 6 over, 10 over, lambi, Yes/Not — that nobody has ever explained to them. This is the explanation.

Published 19 October 2026 · Learning · 7 min read

Quick answer

A session (fancy) bet is a bet on runs scored in a fixed block of overs — say the first 6 overs — against a line the market sets. You bet Yes (the score beats the line) or Not (it does not), usually at prices near even money. Sessions settle the moment the block ends, on the official score, which is why they are fast, popular, and unforgiving of guessing.

The anatomy of a session line

A typical line reads: 6 Over Session — 48 / 50. Two numbers, one decision. The market is saying the innings' first six overs will produce around 48–50 runs. Yes at 50 means you win if the score after six overs is more than 50; Not at 48 means you win if it is less than 48. Land exactly on a boundary number and the market's published rule for that line decides — read it before the bet, not after the argument. Your stake is per-point in most session markets: stake Rs 100 a point and finish 6 runs the right side of the line, and the return is calculated on those 6 points — which is also how a session can cost more than a casual glance suggested. Know your per-point exposure before the block starts; the over does not wait.

The session types you will actually meet

MarketWhat is being pricedSettles
Over blocks (6 / 10 / 15 / 20)Runs in that block of the inningsEnd of the block
Lambi (innings session)The full innings total against a lineEnd of the innings
Ball-by-ball fanciesRuns or events on the next delivery or overImmediately
Fall-of-wicket linesScore at which the next wicket fallsOn the wicket, or innings end
Batsman fanciesAn individual batsman's runs against a lineOn dismissal or innings end

How sessions differ from match odds — and why it matters

Match odds are an exchange market against other players, priced by the crowd, where you can trade out — the whole back and lay machinery. Sessions are priced lines with Yes/Not buttons, settled in minutes, with no trade-out culture and much faster emotional weather. The practical consequences: sessions punish impulsive staking (a bad over moves a line violently), they reward genuine knowledge of a team's powerplay habits, and they end — completely — every few overs, which makes them the easiest market in the building to chase on. The desk's honest advice: if you are learning, learn on match odds first; sessions are a second instrument, not a first one. The broader mechanics live on the BPExch cricket page.

Settlement honesty checks

  • Official score only: sessions settle on the official scorecard feed — a TV graphic error is not a settlement.
  • Shortened matches: a block that cannot be completed is void; a completed block settles even if the match later dies.
  • Retired hurt is not out for batsman fancies unless the market rules say so — the rules tab on the market is the contract; read it once, before your first session on that market.
  • Every settlement is a statement line showing the line, the result and the points — the evidence exists if you ever dispute one.

Sessions are not a scam and not a shortcut; they are a sharp, fast market that pays specialists and taxes tourists. Learn the lines here, watch three matches' worth without staking, and let the how BPExch works page ground the rest before your money joins in.
