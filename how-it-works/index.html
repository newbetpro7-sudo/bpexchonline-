How does a betting exchange work? Back, lay, and the ladder

How does a betting exchange work? Everything reduces to one screen: a ladder of prices other players are offering. Learn to read it and the rest — stakes, liability, trading out — is arithmetic. We will use one over of a PSL match and real-feeling numbers.

Quick answer

On BPExch you back an outcome at a price another player offers, or lay it — betting it will not happen and taking the backer's stake if you are right. Prices move with the match; you can trade out mid-game by taking the opposite side.

The ladder, read properly

Each runner in a market shows a blue box and a pink box. Blue is the best price available to back — bet that it happens. Pink is the best price to lay — bet that it does not. The smaller number under each price is the money waiting there: back Pakistan at 2.14 with Rs 84,200 available and your Rs 1,000 stake is matched instantly, with room to spare. If you ask for more than the available amount, the rest sits unmatched until another player takes it — or the price moves away and it never does.

One over, both sides

Powerplay, Lahore batting, Pakistan — say you fancy the batting side at 2.14 and back them with Rs 1,000. Two quick wickets fall; the price drifts to 3.40. You have three options, and only an exchange gives you all three: let it ride, back again at the bigger price, or lay Pakistan now at 3.45 and lock the position down to a smaller, certain loss (or, if the price had shortened instead, a certain profit either way). That third option — trading out — is the entire reason serious players use exchanges. The numbers, worked rupee by rupee, are in the blog version: back and lay, explained with one PSL over.

Liability: the number that surprises beginners

Backing is simple: your stake is your maximum loss. Laying flips it — you are taking someone else's stake, so your risk is stake × (price − 1). Lay a team at 4.0 for a backer's Rs 1,000 and your liability is Rs 3,000: that is what leaves your balance if the team wins. The interface shows the liability before you confirm; read it every single time. More first-month accounts are dented by an unread liability than by bad tips.

Commission, honestly

The platform charges a small commission on your net winnings in a market — win Rs 10,000 in a market and a few hundred comes off; lose, and there is nothing to commission. It is the price of the marketplace, and it is still usually cheaper than a bookmaker's built-in margin. It is also why the desk that issued your BPExch account earns when you play rather than when you sign up — and why your account was free.

Questions we get asked about this

What does laying a team mean?

Laying means betting that the outcome will NOT happen — you take the backer's stake and pay out at the agreed price if it does. Your risk is the liability (stake × (price − 1)), shown before you confirm.

Why do BPExch prices change during a match?

Because players set them. Every wicket, boundary and rumour reprices what the crowd thinks, and the ladder reflects it within seconds. That movement is the opportunity — and the risk.

What is an unmatched bet?

A bet asking for a price or amount nobody has taken yet. It waits in the market until matched, the price moves, or you cancel it. Unmatched money is not lost; it is simply not yet a bet.

Can I close a bet before the match ends?

Yes — take the opposite side at the current price (lay what you backed, or back what you laid) and the position locks. Depending on how the price moved, you will have secured a profit or trimmed a loss.

What does the money figure under each price mean?

That is the amount available to match at that price right now — the market's depth. Small figures mean your full stake may not match at that price.
